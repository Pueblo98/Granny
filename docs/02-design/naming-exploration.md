# Naming exploration and decision kit

Granny is a temporary repository codename. **No public name, assistant persona or wake word is accepted.** This is an English-language creative exploration and preliminary discovery screen dated 2026-09-13, not trademark/legal/domain clearance. Launch countries and languages are unknown; English is only the proposed first prototype language.

## Naming architecture recommendation

| Architecture | Benefit | Cost / recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Unified company/product masterbrand; assistant described as the product | One name to hear, recall, find and request support for; extends to later platform | Recommended provisionally; avoid claiming a human identity |
| Separate company, product and companion names | Corporate flexibility; natural personal address | Three names burden onboarding/support and may imply a human persona; not recommended for MVP |
| Distinct spoken invocation | Can optimize acoustic detection independent of company name | Defer wake word; tap-to-talk MVP needs no invocation name |

Use chosen masterbrand for app/platform/company where clearance permits; future OS/hardware extensions may use descriptive suffixes only as future-stage context. Do not design those products. If a future wake word needs different phonetics, test it separately rather than forcing today's company name into a detector.

## Criteria before selection

Names must be adult/dignified, warm without gendered/child/clinical framing, pronounceable and spellable after hearing, comfortable in home/support conversations, distinctive in search, and suitable for an ownable wordmark/icon. Avoid helplessness, a specific relative role, age/diagnosis label or AI/robot cliché. Test voice recognition against household speech and common homophones; a short name is not automatically a good wake word. Extend beyond a tablet without promising future capabilities. Check cultural and linguistic interpretation in selected markets and with both adult users and buyers.

**Scoring:** 1 poor/high risk → 5 strong/low risk. Weighting totals 100: pronunciation 12, spelling 8, recall 8, warmth 10, dignity 12, distinctiveness 12, voice use 10, cross-generation 6, extensibility 8, international risk 5, visual potential 4, collision risk 5. Weighted score = Σ(weight × score)/5. These are analyst judgments, not measured user/acoustic/market results. Collision is also a knockout: a high total cannot rescue a direct conflicting product.

## Recommended finalists for Simon's iteration

### Daykind — everyday warmth and capability

Pronunciation DAY-kynd. Story: technology should make an ordinary day more manageable while treating its user kindly. “I use Daykind on my tablet.” “Daykind, show me the photos.” Company/product fit strong; companion address plausible without pretending to be human; wake-word fit untested. Wordmark can emphasize open d/k counters, symbol a daylight opening rather than a sun face; app icon a simple open frame. Pairs with Open Day and Everyday Spark.

**For:** adult, memorable, broad daily value, no diagnosis/relative role. **Against:** kindness/wellness moralizing and two-word spelling; no domain/mark evidence. Ask adults what it does from name alone, whether saying it feels natural, and whether it sounds patronizing. Ask helpers to repeat/spell after hearing once. Simon could keep “ordinary day” direction while removing sentimental wording; don't generate twenty day+suffix variants.

### Handspan — a useful computer within reach

Pronunciation HAND-span. Story: the scale of a human action, with control remaining in the person's hands. “Can you help me set up Handspan?” “I asked Handspan to open the music.” Strong company/product fit; companion address more tool-like, which may be welcome; weak wake-word candidate until speech tests. Wordmark uses sturdy open letters; symbol could be two measured endpoints/open span, never a helping-hand caregiver logo. Pairs with Open Day or Bright Signal.

**For:** concrete adult agency, physical recognition, extensible without age coding. **Against:** hand/touch emphasis could exclude voice/motor-access users; travel/other business uses require review. Ask whether it implies hardware only, what the hand metaphor means to different access profiles, and whether it sounds like an aid imposed on someone. Simon can explore “human scale/reach” without literal hand imagery.

### Clearfold — complexity made understandable

Pronunciation KLEER-fohld. Story: layers of computing folded into one clear surface and decision. “Clearfold opened the right conversation.” “I need help with Clearfold on my tablet.” Strong platform/company fit, deliberately less person-like companion; wake word remains untested. Adaptable folded-window wordmark/symbol and broad geometric app icon. Pairs with Bright Signal or Living Pages.

**For:** direct fit to screen understanding and organized, transparent action. **Against:** existing paper mark requires specialist review; can sound like document/foldable hardware product; colder than Daykind. Ask whether users expect a file tool, what “fold” suggests, and whether control remains apparent. Simon can choose clarity/structure as a direction while rejecting the exact name. **Do not adopt the string before mark review.**

Recommendation order for creative testing: Daykind, Handspan, Clearfold. These are three distinct strategic positions, not three cleared names. No finalist is ready for public launch. The codename stays until Simon explicitly selects an approved replacement.
