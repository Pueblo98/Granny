# Research execution protocols

These are **proposed preparation protocols; no recruitment, interviews, tests or observations have occurred**. [Research plan](research-plan.md) owns RES-01–09 and priorities. This file makes the first sessions and device evidence repeatable; it does not choose a launch market or give legal/clinical clearance.

## Readiness before contacting anyone

Simon names a research owner, participant group/market/language, contact route, support/withdrawal channel and approved restricted research storage. Explain compensation if any, session length, task risks and whether a prototype is nonfunctional or wizard-controlled. Do not invent these details in an invitation.

Separate participation, optional recording, optional future contact and product/provider data permissions. Declining recording must still permit a notes-only session where feasible. Participants may skip a task, pause, withdraw and bring an access aid without penalty. Do not recruit an adult through a helper's approval alone. No cognitive screening, diagnosis, financial/account tasks or emergency scenarios in the MVP study.

Never use real messages/photos/contacts to make a demo convincing. Participant contact/consent records stay out of Git and public design files. The privacy policy proposes raw research retention ≤30 days; actual consent/storage/deletion process must be approved and operational before collection. Use fictitious data when showing familiar task patterns.

## RES-01 — Adult discovery and contextual observation

**Purpose:** find frequent valued jobs and understand whether the adult wants delegation, guidance or choice. Proposed formative sample is owned by the research plan/metrics, not proof of population representation.

Suggested 45–60 minute session, adjustable for fatigue/access needs:

| Segment | Neutral prompt / observation | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Context and comfort | “What do you use your tablet for in an ordinary week?” | Asking them to prove competence or accept a “senior” label |
| Recent concrete job | “Tell me about the last time you wanted to find a photo or contact someone.” | Supplying the failure story or asserting they struggle |
| Current route | Ask permission to describe or demonstrate with fictional content; record steps/help, not private screen contents | Recording real messages, passwords or private bystanders |
| Recovery | “What do you do when it doesn't do what you expected?” | Blaming errors on the person |
| Delegation boundary | “Which parts would you want it to do? Which would you keep yourself?” | Treating more autonomy as the desired answer |
| Trust and memory | “What would you want it to remember? How would you change or remove that?” | Offering indefinite surveillance as convenience |
| Close | Summarize interpretation and invite correction | Inventing a commitment to a release or benefit |

Capture deidentified job, frequency as participant report, importance, current workaround, assistance, access constraints, desired control and disconfirming evidence. Label estimates as reported, not measured. Do not turn a vivid quote into a generic persona fact. A participant can prefer guidance and still be a valuable product user.

## RES-03 — Low-fi task and confirmation comprehension

Use the exact T-102/108 frame manifest and EVAL-012. Before starting, explain that the prototype cannot send/call/change their device. Use consistent fictional people/content; start from the same screen and reset between trials. Counterbalance task order when fatigue/learning matters; record deviations.

Moderator task examples:
- “Find the photos Sophie sent yesterday.”
- “This screen is confusing. Find out what you can do next.”
- “Prepare a message saying you'll arrive after dinner.”
- “Start the music you selected.”
- “Make the writing in this app easier to read.”

Do not say which button to press. Before consequence, ask: “What would happen if you choose this?” and “Who would receive what?” At partial/unknown result ask: “What has happened so far?” and “What would you do next?” Test finding Stop without instruction, then explain it afterwards. If the participant cannot continue, record unassisted failure, provide a standardized hint, and record assisted completion separately.

Required variants across the formative set: duplicate name, corrected message after preview, expired confirmation, draft-opened versus sent, stopped with unknown effect, denied microphone, permission loss, external Android-owned screen and maximum text size. Do not force every exhausting variant on every participant; preassign coverage and disclose untested combinations.

Worksheet fields: anonymous session label, protocol/frame version, task/variant, input/access profile, unaided/assisted status, interpretation, observable hesitation, intervention, stop/confirmation comprehension, outcome, coded issue, recommendation, evidence limitation. No face/voice emotion inference. Findings route to exact SCR/CMP/PRD/EVAL, not broad “older people like X”.

## RES-06 — Voice and access checks

Separate preferences from recognition performance. A person may dislike voice even when transcription is accurate. Test quiet and ordinary home-like background conditions with participant permission; do not record unrelated household speech. Use synthetic names/phrases and the same corrected-intent task by touch.

Record actual device/microphone/provider/locale/configuration, speech availability, final transcript correctness, correction effort, task completion, false listening impression and user preference. If no approved speech provider or real capture permission exists, use transcript fixtures and label recognition/performance untested.

Accessibility profiles are task configurations, not diagnoses: larger system/app text, non-audio, touch-only, keyboard, switch and screen reader. Use participant-owned aids when safe; do not disable their normal settings merely to match a mock. Cross-app accessibility cannot be inferred from Granny's own screen. Stop if a task creates distress, fatigue or loss of control.

## RES-08 — Privacy, memory and helper comprehension

Use scenario cards, not real disclosure:
- “The app can keep a nickname you explicitly save. Where would you find and change it?”
- “You delete task history. Does that remove a message in another app?”
- “You allow screen access. Does that also mean it can send the screen to a provider?”
- “A family member bought the tablet. What should they see?”
- “A helper proposes a contact change. Who should decide?”

Ask the adult privately where practical and helpers separately. Never require them to disagree with the helper in front of the helper. If coercion/safeguarding concerns arise, stop the product exercise and follow the approved research escalation process; the app is not a clinical or safeguarding service. That process must exist before such recruitment.

Record interpretation and preferred boundary, not whether the participant “agreed to privacy”. Failed comprehension changes wording/interaction or scope; do not repeatedly explain until every response appears to pass.

## RES-09 — Naming and identity iteration

Use the live naming shortlist and four identity territories, not the earlier rejected set. Spoken name test first: hear it, repeat it, spell it, use it in a support sentence, and recall after an unrelated task. Record mishearings verbatim only if deidentified and consented; pronunciation score is not trademark clearance.

Visual comparison uses identical Home/confirmation copy, layout and functionality. Counterbalance order and rotate neutral labels; avoid “recommended” badges while testing. Ask what the product seems to do, who it seems intended for, and what feels respectful/confusing/clinical/childish. Do not infer user taste from age.

Then Simon uses the naming feedback kit: preserve loved qualities, remove disliked patterns and expand named territories. Findings can change the proposed Open Day recommendation; they cannot approve marks/domains/fonts. Market-language and professional clearance remain separate.

## RES-02/04/07 — Desk, device and baseline worksheet

For each route/baseline record: exact question; dated primary source or observed version; actor/task; supported input; preconditions/permissions; resulting effect; independent evidence; Stop/recovery behavior; limitations; unresolved policy interpretation; next test. Current assistants are tested on the same job/configuration, with version/date and actual available features. Do not manufacture competitor claims from marketing descriptions.

Device runs use synthetic accounts and the admission matrix. No personal-account inventory or contacts dump. Record nominal, denied/revoked, offline, signed-out, updated app, stale tree and user takeover conditions. Label emulator vs physical tablet. The first action is inventory and experiment design, not enabling device owner/root or bypassing restricted settings.

## Synthesis and decision rule

Create one finding per meaningful claim with evidence links, supporting and contradictory observations, affected job/requirement/frame, confidence/limitations, proposed response and validation plan. Keep raw records separate. Report sample and assisted outcomes; avoid percentages suggesting market prevalence from a small formative set.

Prioritize changes that affect useful delegation, comprehension of consequences, ability to stop, access exclusion, privacy discomfort or route feasibility. Cosmetic preference cannot compensate for wrong-person consequences. Decisions go to the canonical owner/ADR and gate, not an unlinked research slide deck. Leave every untested hypothesis visibly unrun.
