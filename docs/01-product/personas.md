# Personas and Jobs to Be Done

## Evidence warning

These are **proto-personas**, synthesized from the Dream Book and planning conversation. They guide questions; they are not validated user segments and must not be cited as research.

## Barbara — leading primary hypothesis

An independent older adult, approximately mid-70s, who is comfortable speaking and using a few familiar apps but dislikes changing interfaces, settings, passwords, popups, and recovery. She values family connection and continuity, may have ordinary age-related vision/dexterity changes, and does not want technology to become the topic.

Jobs:

- When I know the outcome I want, let me ask for it without knowing the app or menu.
- When a screen changes or becomes confusing, help me continue without embarrassment.
- Help me stay connected to important people and content.
- Remember useful relationship and preference context so I do not constantly repeat myself.

## George — access-needs hypothesis

An independent older adult with reduced vision, hearing, or dexterity. He tests whether Granny's core experience remains usable through flexible type, strong contrast, forgiving touch, clear audio/visual states, and sufficient time—without treating access needs as cognitive incapacity.

## Alice — trusted-family hypothesis

Barbara's adult daughter who lives at a distance. She wants to configure trusted contacts and help with genuine technical issues while respecting Barbara's independence and private communications.

Jobs:

- Help my parent benefit from technology without providing constant interface support.
- Know what limited assistance I can provide and when Barbara can see or revoke it.
- Contribute family context or content only within explicit permissions.

## Robert — future advanced-support hypothesis

A potential future user with more complex cognitive or care needs. **Not an assumed MVP user:** serving this group could introduce medical, consent, safeguarding, and reliability obligations that have not been defined.

## Anti-assumptions

- Age does not imply cognitive impairment, loneliness, poor judgment, identical family structures, or unwillingness to learn.
- “Older adults” is not a single market segment.
- Family involvement is optional and must not override the primary user's authority.

## Research needed

- Recruit representative independent older adults and family helpers across ability, language, household, and technology-experience ranges.
- Test whether the leading job and target setting are accurate.
- Understand current workarounds, trust thresholds, privacy boundaries, and device habits before accepting persona details.
